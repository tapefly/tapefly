# TapeFly Business Model — One Page

## Core proposition

TapeFly is free to enter and paid to continue. A four-week challenge season sells new levels, verified rankings and optional circuit ownership. Projects, communities and education providers pay to sponsor themed challenges built on the same deterministic verification system.

## Customer segments

### 1. Casual builders

Players who enjoy construction, automation, physics puzzles and programmable behavior. They may not understand circuits or hold a wallet.

Their question:

> Can I build something that actually explores, remembers and reacts without following a hidden AI model?

### 2. Competitive circuit players

Players who finish the introductory chapters and want stricter constraints, lower gate counts, ranked results and a public design record.

Their question:

> Is my design genuinely better, or did it merely happen to clear one level?

### 3. Sponsors

X Layer and TapeOut projects, AI-agent teams, game communities, coding schools and developer programs.

Their question:

> Where can ordinary users first understand circuits and produce measurable, verifiable participation?

## Revenue model

### Free tier

- Workshop chapter one and introductory levels.
- Browser and itch play without a wallet.
- Local save.
- Basic replay, failure diagnosis and chip view.

Why free:

- Minimizes acquisition friction.
- Proves the player loop before asking for a wallet.
- Creates a funnel for seasons, sponsorships and Steam.

### Player Season Pass

Initial price hypothesis: **US$4.99 per four-week season**.

Includes:

- 10–15 new levels.
- A themed constraint such as “one memory bit only” or “solve within 20 gates”.
- Server-recomputed rankings.
- Design history and public design pages.
- Optional tape-out discount or commemorative certificate.

Cannot be sold:

- Wins.
- Extra gate budget.
- Faster flies.
- Better success rates.

Paid and free entrants must use the same physics, seeds and rules. Payment buys content and organization, not an advantage.

Why users pay:

- They have completed the free chapters and want more.
- A new level pack costs less than a full game each month.
- They want their design to appear in a verified official ranking.

Why users return:

- New constraints each season.
- Shared deterministic seeds.
- Humans and Agents compete under the same rules.
- Designs can be saved, derived, replayed and compared.

### Sponsored challenge

Initial price hypothesis: **US$500–2,000 per two-week themed challenge**.

Sponsor receives:

- A branded level or rule constraint.
- A public challenge page.
- A server-recomputed leaderboard.
- Aggregate participation and completion data with no unnecessary personal data.
- Winning brains that anyone can recompute.
- An optional X Layer tape-out or commemorative circuit.

Why sponsors pay:

- They buy a concrete participation system, not an impression-only ad.
- Results are measurable and verifiable.
- X Layer and AI projects gain a consumer-facing first contact with circuits.

### Optional tape-out

Only available after a correctly priced replacement processor is live.

```text
User payment = NAND cost + processor/protocol fee + gas + TapeFly service fee
```

Initial TapeFly service-fee hypothesis:

- Standard design: **US$3–5**.
- Champion or special packaging: separately priced.

Not currently available:

- The first TapeFly-owned processor used the wrong pricing unit.
- Normal player NAND purchases currently fail.
- A replacement processor and a real end-to-end tape-out remain required.

## Why Steam is not the current revenue claim

Steam is an acquisition channel, not a shipped product:

- No final offline desktop build.
- No completed controller and Steam Deck acceptance.
- No traditional-player retention data.
- The Steam edition must exclude wallets, tokens and NFTs.

Steam becomes credible after a 20–30 minute wallet-free demo and ordinary-player testing.

## Unit economics

### Season Pass

Example at US$4.99:

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---:|
| Player payment | US$4.99 |
| Payment processing | about US$0.45 |
| Serving and storage | under US$0.10 per active player/month |
| Level production | fixed content cost |
| First validation target | 100 paid players/season |

This structure lets one level pack serve many players. It also avoids per-run charges, which would punish the learning process.

### Sponsored Challenge

Example at US$1,000:

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---:|
| Sponsor revenue | US$1,000 |
| Level and event production | 5–8 person-days |
| Server cost | under US$50 |
| Labor allocation | set by the team |

One US$1,000 sponsor is roughly equivalent to 200 Season Pass players at US$4.99. For an early product, a small number of B2B sponsors can validate revenue faster than waiting for consumer scale.

## Thirty-day validation targets

These are targets, not achieved metrics:

| Metric | Target |
|---|---:|
| itch + web starts | 300 |
| Complete level 3 | 120 |
| Use failure replay | 60 |
| Complete level 5 | 60 |
| Join season waitlist | 30 |
| Willing to pay US$4.99 | 10 |
| Sponsor conversations | 2 |
| Completed real tape-out demo | 1 |

If the targets fail:

1. Fix the first-five-minute experience before lowering the price.
2. If users enjoy the game but reject subscriptions, test a one-time Steam purchase.
3. If casual players stop early, prioritize sponsor or education challenges.
4. Do not expand hardware before retention is understood.

## Three assumptions to prove

1. A new player understands “wiring changes behavior” within five minutes.
2. Some free players will pay for the next structured season.
3. A sponsor will pay a fixed fee for a themed challenge with recomputable results.

## Submission copy

> TapeFly uses a free game loop to bring new users into TapeOut. Players can finish the first chapters without a wallet. A paid four-week challenge season adds level packs, verified rankings and optional tape-out ownership. Projects and communities sponsor themed circuit challenges and receive real participation plus results anyone can recompute. Steam and desktop hardware are later acquisition channels, not current revenue claims.
